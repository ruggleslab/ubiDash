Integrated multi-omic analysis of Cullin-RING E3 ligases in cancer

Proper regulation of protein stoichiometry is vital for maintaining cellular homeostasis. E3 ubiquitin ligases (E3s) play pivotal roles in targeting substrate proteins for degradation through the Ubiquitin Proteasome System (UPS). The human genome encodes for over 600 E3s. In cancer, genomic mutations often lead to dysfunction of the UPS. However, the extent to which E3s are involved in tumor initiation and progression is still unclear. Much of the biochemical work to characterize these has focused on less than 20% of E3s, leaving the remaining unexplored for their roles in cancer. Computational studies have started to investigate the function of E3s in cancer at the genome and transcriptome level, suggesting that over 19% of cancer drivers comprise components of the UPS. Here, I propose to systematically analyze the role of E3s across multiple cancer cohorts at the proteome level in silico, followed by in vitro validation to elucidate the biological mechanism of specific E3s to uncover novel therapeutic strategies. Leveraging the Ruggles Lab expertise in computational cancer research and the Pagano Lab expertise in the UPS field, I seek to study multi-omics data from the Clinical Proteomics Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC) and the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) to characterize the role of E3s in cancer. Given the strong evidence of E3s acting as oncoproteins and tumor suppressors, I hypothesize that E3s are promising therapeutic targets that modulate key players in cancer development and synergize with existing therapeutic modalities.
