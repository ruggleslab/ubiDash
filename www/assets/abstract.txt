Cells rely on the ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) to remove damaged proteins and fine-tune critical cellular processes. Central to this machinery are E3 ligases—enzymes that dictate specificity and recruitment of proteins for ubiquitylation. In cancer, E3 ligases are often hijacked, mutated, or misregulated, contributing to tumor growth and treatment resistance. By integrating large-scale proteogenomic datasets from patient tumors and cancer models, we apply supervised learning to develop statistical models that reveal how mutations and signaling changes reshape E3-substrate interactions across cancer types. Our analyses highlight key vulnerabilities in the UPS, including E3 ligases with potential as therapeutic entry points for next-generation approaches such as targeted protein degradation. <span style="color:#6A2798; font-weight:bold;">To support community exploration, we developed UbiDash, an interactive online resource for navigating the cancer UPS landscape.</span> This work lays the foundation for precision oncology strategies that exploit the cell’s own protein disposal system.
